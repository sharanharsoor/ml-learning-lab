Switch to 21 different measured Spambase emails. X is the longest uppercase run. Z is the number of exclamation marks per 1,000 characters, computed by multiplying UCI’s character percentage by 10. For instance, Z = 9.25 means 0.925% of the characters are exclamation marks.

These rows were chosen to make a two-cut boundary visible. For example, M8 is Legitimate even with a capitals run of 4 and 9.25 exclamations per 1,000 characters. The marker shows the observed label. Shading shows the tree’s prediction.

Click or drag to preview a cut. The dashed line snaps to a midpoint between observed values in the selected region, rounded to two decimals here. Apply the cut to ask whether the measurement is at most that cutoff. Select a child region to make the next cut there.

Where do the two worked cutoffs come from?

The first cutoff is 5, halfway between the observed capitals runs of 4 and 6. Among emails on the higher side of that cut, the relevant observed exclamation rates are 3.75 and 6.28. Their midpoint is 5.015, shown as 5.02 on this chart.
