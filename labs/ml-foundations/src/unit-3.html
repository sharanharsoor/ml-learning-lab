How would a model use a weather category?

We can make a yes-or-no feature for each group. For the observed code 2, a weather_group_2 column is 1 and a weather_group_1 column is 0. This is called indicator encoding. Use weather information that would be available when the forecast is made.

Describe the center and spread

How do we calculate each statistic?

Let n be the number of counts, xᵢ one count, and x̄ the mean. The symbol ∑ tells us to add across all n counts.

  • Mean describes the result of sharing the total evenly. x̄ = (∑ xᵢ) ÷ n.
  • Median is the middle of the sorted counts. With an even number of counts, average the two middle values.
  • Variance is the mean squared distance from the mean. Variance = [∑ (xᵢ − x̄)²] ÷ n. Signed distances sum to zero, so squaring keeps them from canceling and gives larger distances more weight.
  • Standard deviation = √variance. The square root brings the unit back from rentals² to rentals, so the spread can be read on the same scale as the counts.

In the measured table, the 08:00 count is 779. Its distance from the mean is 779 − 416 = 363 rentals. Its contribution to the sum above is 363² = 131,769 rentals².

Why divide the variance by five here?

We describe the spread of these exact five hours, so we average their five squared distances. In a statistics lesson that estimates a wider population’s variance from a random sample, the usual sample variance uses n − 1. These five consecutive hours are a descriptive slice of one morning.

When would I use the median?

Toggle the what-if and watch the mean move while the middle place in the sorted list stays the same. A median can describe a typical observation or serve as a baseline when large counts pull the mean upward. The forecasting example in this course uses the mean of historical records for the same hour and day type.

Why inspect workdays and non-workdays separately?

At 08:00, the two groups in these training records have different distributions. A single shared average would mix them. The day-type comparison motivates a forecasting feature we can know in advance. It also helps us spot which subgroups have fewer examples.

Does the scatterplot prove that temperature changes demand?

It shows recorded pairs for this date range. Temperature moves with season and weather, and the number of riders can also change over time. To estimate the effect of temperature itself, we would need a study that accounts for these other differences.

Check what the dataset recorded

An unusually high or low count is a clue to investigate. Compare it with similar hours, check the source, and record your decision before modeling. Rush hour can make a high count entirely plausible.

What do I do if I find a data problem?

Confirm what the field or row represents. If a measured target is missing, look for a source that can supply it before using that observation as a labeled training example. For missing inputs, a simple starting point is to fill numerical values with the training median and record which values were missing. For repeats, confirm the real unit of observation before deduplicating. Document the reason for any correction.
