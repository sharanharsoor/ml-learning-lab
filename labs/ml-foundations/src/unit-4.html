Test whether a model generalizes

We forecast 470 rentals for 8 a.m. on November 1, 2012. Capital Bikeshare recorded 680. Our forecast missed by 210 rentals. Would the same method work across many future hours?

Learn from the past, then test on later hours

Generalization means making useful predictions on new rows outside the training set. We keep the order of the calendar because the job is to predict future rentals.

  1. 1. Train Jan 2011 to Sep 2012
  2. 2. Validate Oct 2012
  3. 3. Test Nov to Dec 2012

Fit two candidate rules

  • Overall mean baseline gives every hour the same forecast. It rounds the average of all training counts.
  • Calendar model groups training rows by hour and workday status. It rounds the average within the matching group.

A baseline sets a reference point. We can ask how much prediction error the calendar model saves over a rule that ignores the hour.

Choose using validation error

Forecast October using the rules fitted through September. For each hour, take the absolute difference between prediction and actual. Divide the total miss by the number of hours to get mean absolute error, or MAE. Lower is better.

The rule is locked

The calendar model was selected using October. Its group averages now use January 2011 through October 31, 2012. The baseline uses the same rows and forecasts 189 rentals for every test hour.

At 08:00 on November 1 we know it is a workday. The calendar forecast is round(214,195 ÷ 456) = 470 rentals. Every count in that average comes from before November 1.

The calendar model misses by about 63.52 rentals per hour on average over this test period. Compare that with the 210-rental miss we saw at 8 a.m. A single miss and an average miss answer different questions.

Why can we train again on October after choosing?

October had two jobs in sequence. First its labels checked the candidates fitted through September. Once the calendar rule won, October became extra historical training data for estimating the group averages. November and December still stayed outside both steps.

Why can October have a higher error than the test months?

They cover different dates. November and December have a different mix of rental counts and the final rules are refitted on more history. The difference in errors alone cannot tell us how much each factor contributed.

Choose a split that matches how predictions will be used

Decide what must be new at test time. Select a scenario to see what should be kept apart.

    Can two split requirements apply together?

    Yes. Repeated emails from the same campaign might need to stay together while each split still contains enough spam to measure recall. Group integrity comes first. With a forecasting task, the final test must remain later in time even if class proportions change.

    Would this information be available at 7:59 a.m.?

    Data leakage happens when unavailable information slips into training, prediction, or model selection. Use the same recorded hour to test a shortcut.

    Recorded total
    Absolute error

    The source file records totals for casual and registered riders. Their sum gives the target by definition. It is a valid accounting identity after the hour and a leaky input before the hour. UCI Bike Sharing dataset and data dictionary

    How do duplicates and preprocessing leak information?
    • Duplicate rows. A copied test email in training lets a model see the same example and its label early. Drop verified duplicates or keep related copies in one split before evaluating.
    • Preprocessing. If you fill missing training values with a mean computed over all rows, that mean already depends on future rows. Fit the mean, category encoding, or scaling rule on training rows and apply the fitted rule to validation and test rows.
    • Repeated testing. Trying many models and selecting the one with the best test result uses that test for model selection. Make those choices on validation and reserve an independent final check.

    The notebook reads all 17,379 records, fits both rules, and runs this sequence. Each mean uses the rows passed to fit_rules.

    candidates = fit_rules(train)

chosen = min(validation_errors, key=validation_errors.get)

final = fit_rules(train + validation)
test_errors = evaluate(
    {chosen: final[chosen], "Overall mean": final["Overall mean"]},
