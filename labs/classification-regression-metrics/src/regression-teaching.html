Same average miss, different consequences

All five arrivals stay fixed. Compare five misses of 5 minutes with one miss of 25 minutes. Absolute errors add up to the same total. Squared errors react more strongly to the large miss.

Can positive and negative misses cancel?

With the same arrivals, imagine the app predicts five minutes early for the first order and five minutes late for the second. The other predictions are exact.

Ask how much squared error the model saves

Compare SSE, the model’s sum of squared residuals, with SST, the sum of squared distances from the mean of the actual arrivals. Both totals use the same observations.

What if every delivery takes the same amount of time?

Then every actual value equals its mean, so SST is zero and the usual R² ratio has no numerical value. MAE and RMSE can still describe the misses.

Pick the metric that answers your question

What size miss should I expect?
MAE averages the absolute miss in minutes. Each missed minute adds the same amount.
Do big misses matter more?
MSE averages squared misses in minutes². RMSE takes its square root and reports the result in minutes.
How much better is this than the mean reference?
R² gives the relative reduction in squared error. Zero matches the reference and a negative value means worse. It is unitless.
Are our estimates systematically high or low?
Mean residual shows average direction. Pair it with an error-size metric because positive and negative residuals can cancel.

The evaluation-target mean is the mathematical reference for R². To compare against a deployable constant predictor, fit that predictor on training data and evaluate it on held-out deliveries.
