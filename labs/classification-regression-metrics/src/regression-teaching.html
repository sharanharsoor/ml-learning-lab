How far off was our arrival estimate?

Order E01 leaves at 12:00. Asha’s app gives the customer an estimated arrival time (ETA) of 12:25, which is a forecast of 25 minutes. The courier records delivery at 12:20. Asha can now subtract the dispatch time to get the actual duration of 20 minutes.

An ETA model could use the planned route distance and traffic known at dispatch. Here, we supply 25 minutes as an example forecast and start at the evaluation step.

Follow the forecast to the actual arrival

Each of the five example rows (E01 to E05) pairs a forecast made at dispatch with the duration measured after arrival. Every value is in minutes since that order left. The classification lesson uses a different set of orders (D01 to D20) to ask whether a promised deadline was met.

Once the order arrives, compute residual = actual duration − predicted duration. A negative result means the app estimated too many minutes. A positive result means it estimated too few.

Ask how much squared error the model saves

Compare the sum of squared errors (SSE) from the selected estimates with the total sum of squares (SST) from using the mean observed duration for all five orders. Only the selected estimates change.

The mean gives the smallest squared error among constant guesses on these rows. R² builds its reference from the durations observed after delivery. To evaluate a constant predictor you could use at dispatch, choose its value from training data before scoring it here.

What if every delivery takes the same amount of time?

In a separate three-order example, actual durations are 40, 40, and 40 minutes and the forecasts are 35, 40, and 45. The mean actual duration is 40, so SST is zero. The usual R² formula divides by zero and gives no finite value. MAE is still (5 + 0 + 5) ÷ 3, about 3.33 minutes.

Pick the metric that answers your question

How far off were these estimates on average?
MAE averages the absolute miss in minutes. Each missed minute adds the same amount.
Do big misses matter more?
MSE averages squared misses in minutes². RMSE takes its square root and reports the result in minutes.
How much better is this than the mean reference?
R² gives the relative reduction in squared error. Zero matches the reference and a negative value means worse. It is unitless.
Are our estimates systematically high or low?
Mean residual shows average direction. Pair it with an error-size metric because positive and negative residuals can cancel.

The evaluation-target mean is the mathematical reference for R². To compare against a deployable constant predictor, fit that predictor on training data and evaluate it on held-out deliveries.
